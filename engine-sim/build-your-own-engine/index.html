How a spec becomes a simulation

Geometry first, then the cycle, then the dyno. Nothing on this page is a canned curve.

01

Geometry sets the airflow

Bore, stroke and cylinder count decide how much air the engine can move per revolution. That displacement, plus the compression ratio, is the input every other number is built on.

02

The cycle does the rest

The simulator steps the engine cycle by cycle, with induction, fuel, ignition and exhaust resolved at each step. Change the compression ratio and the torque curve moves because the physics moved.

03

Measure it, don't guess

Run it on the dyno, watch the power and torque curves build, and listen to the exhaust the model synthesises from the firing order you chose.

The arithmetic on this page

Two formulae, no hidden correction factors.

Displacement
V = π/4 · bore² · stroke · cylinders

Swept volume for one cylinder, multiplied out. The rotary sheet uses 654 cc per rotor instead, so a two-rotor comes out at 1.3 litres the way Mazda badged it.

Mean piston speed
v̄ = 2 · stroke · rpm / 60

The average speed of the piston at the redline you set. Production engines live under roughly 25 m/s; the readout turns amber past that, which is where you are asking for exotic metallurgy.
